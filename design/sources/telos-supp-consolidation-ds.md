## B. Nearly covered — defects in existing tests

**`provide`** — Its test requires an “artifact.” It fails to reach the many pass groups built around live events, performances, spectacles, and experiential productions. Acts in `performance`, `recreation`, parts of `creation`, and `produce` bring an occasion into being without producing an artifact. Narrowest change: *“bring an artifact or a performed event/occasion into being, or keep one functional and available.”* This does not disturb the line against `communicate`: those transmit content; `provide` covers the event as something brought into being.

**`protect`** — Its test requires “a barrier against an identified hazard.” That excludes active firefighting, rescue, evacuation, and direct hazard suppression. Groups such as `public-safety` and many `safety` entries contain emergency response that reduces exposure to a hazard but does not install, monitor, or enforce a barrier. Narrowest change: *“install, monitor, or enforce a barrier, or directly intervene to reduce exposure to an identified hazard.”* The discriminating question against `care` remains intact: `care` operates on the person; `protect` operates on the hazard.

**`judge`** — Its test requires “a contested claim.” Many legal-purpose groups named `justice`, `adjudication`, and `adjudicate` include binding determinations of rights, status, or obligations that are not bilateral disputes: probate, protective orders, some status declarations, and regulatory approvals. Narrowest change: *“resolve a claim or question of legal rights, status, or obligations through a procedure whose determination binds the parties.”* This keeps the discriminating question against `conform`: `conform` tests against an external standard; `judge` produces a binding determination.

## C. Not covered — candidate roots

**`govern`**  
The act adopts, amends, repeals, or authoritatively applies a rule, policy, or institutional structure binding on a collective. Its outward test: *Does the act set, change, or repeal a rule or standing decision for a collective, rather than only comply with, coordinate under, or resolve a dispute under existing rules?*

Discriminating question against nearest neighbours: `conform` applies an external standard; `govern` creates or changes the standard. `judge` resolves a contested claim under rules; `govern` makes or amends the rules. `coordinate` fixes time, place, sequence, or role; `govern` fixes the rule itself. `belong` changes who may enter or act; `govern` changes the rules that say who may and what counts.

This was independently found by roughly twelve of the fifteen passes under names such as `governance`, `govern`, `regulation`, and `order`. Example acts: X0017, X0027, X0043; X0278, X0286, X0312; X0209, X0264, X0268. I tried `conform`, `judge`, `coordinate`, `belong`, and `transfer`; none tests whether the act is changing the collective rule itself.

## D. Rejected candidates

- **`connection` / `community` / `engage` / `connect`** — looked like an affiliation root. Killed by the horizon screen and by existing `belong`/`unite`: informal bonds often require another party’s acceptance or recognition, so the act does not by itself determine the relationship. Where it does, it is usually already a bounded group or a formal registered bond.
- **`influence` / `promotion` / `persuasion`** — killed by the horizon screen. The act does not determine another person’s attitude or uptake; the child `promote` already captures the checkable sponsor/placement/call-to-action structure without requiring the mental effect.
- **`stewardship` / `maintenance` / `continuity` / `upkeep` / `administer` / `manage`** — killed by the mechanism screen or the existing deliberate exclusion of `maintain`. Keeping an artifact or facility available is `provide`; organizing people and roles is `coordinate`/`belong`/`govern`.
- **`exploit` / `subversion` / `transgress`** — killed by the ultimate-purpose screen. They name violations, not a distinct telos. Where value moves, `transfer`/`trade` already covers the economic end; the wrongfulness is a different axis.
- **`autonomy` / `self-direction`** — killed by the standing/horizon screen. The act does not by itself determine self-direction; it is a condition of the actor, not an outcome fixed by mechanism.
- **`documentation` / `recordkeeping`** — killed by the mechanism screen. They serve `know`, `conform`, or `communicate`.
- **`representation` / `counsel` / `staff support`** — killed by the standing-relation screen.

## E. What this corpus still cannot tell you

The passes overwhelmingly exercise legal, administrative, commercial, and platform acts. A general taxonomy would need roots these slices did not genuinely test.

- **`destroy` / `harm` as an intended end** — deliberate demolition, sabotage, punishment, or destruction of value, bodies, or records. None of these passages used destruction as a positive telos; it appeared only as a hazard to be prevented. A criminal-sentencing, war, demolition, or waste-management corpus would exercise it.
- **`worship` / `ritual` / `venerate`** — acts aimed at being conventionally correct toward the sacred, dead, or ceremonial order. The only near miss was pallbearer arrangement, left unplaced. A religious, funerary, or civic-ceremony corpus would test whether this is a root or a case of `conform`/`provide`.
- **`rest` / `recover` / intrinsic play`** — acts whose point is the state of the actor rather than an external output. Recreation appeared mostly as staged entertainment, not unstructured play or recovery. A leisure, early-childhood, or rehabilitation corpus would exercise it.
- **`procreate` / `give birth` / `raise`** — family groups here were legal status and support obligations, not biosocial reproduction or childrearing as an end. A family or demographic corpus would exercise it.
- **`coerce` / `deceive` as technique rather than legal wrong** — fraud and subversion appeared mainly as illegitimate acts. A corpus of interrogation, marketing, diplomacy, or manipulation would test whether these are a root or only means to other ends.
