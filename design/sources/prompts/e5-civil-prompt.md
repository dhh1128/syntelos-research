You are stress-testing a proposed taxonomy against civil-law doctrine. Answer from your own knowledge of the civil-law tradition; no corpus is attached. Where you are not confident a doctrine says what you claim, mark it UNCERTAIN rather than asserting it.

THE PROPOSAL UNDER TEST. "Syntelos 2.0" proposes to identify any act — including legal acts — as a coordinate over these facets:

- **telos**: a hierarchical tree of PURPOSE. Current roots (inherited from a prior version built only for two-party digital interactions, and expected to change): /relate /share /care /serve /align /trade /operate /govern.
- **effect**: closed set of 5 — observe, create, modify, preserve, destroy.
- **state-kind**: closed set of 6 — information, record, commitment, authority, resource, relationship.
- **modality**: may / must / must-not, with omission a first-class act.
- **channel** (closed, claimed jurisdiction-invariant): private, registry, agency, tribunal, assembly.
- **requisites** (open predicate set): writing, signature, witnessed, notarized, filed, recorded, published, noticed, certified.
- Jurisdiction-specific instrument names (e.g. "financing-statement") are NOT facet values; they live in per-jurisdiction profiles.
- An act is a VECTOR: it may occupy several (effect, state-kind) cells at once, and its authorization gate is the strictest any cell demands.

The design was derived almost entirely from common-law sources: the Utah Code, the US Restatement (Third) of Agency, Hohfeld, Hart, and a set of software delegation credentials. The authors claim the closed facets are jurisdiction-invariant. THAT CLAIM IS WHAT YOU ARE TESTING. Be adversarial. A finding that the claim is false is more valuable than a finding that it holds.

3. THE ABSTRACTION PRINCIPLE. German law's Abstraktionsprinzip separates the obligational act (Verpflichtungsgeschäft) from the disposition (Verfügungsgeschäft) — a sale is two juridical acts, not one, and the disposition's validity is independent of the obligation's. French law by contrast transfers property by consent (solo consensu). Can the proposal's (effect, state-kind) vector represent one transaction as two doctrinally independent acts with independent validity? If it cannot, say so plainly and say what breaks. This is the sharpest single test in the audit — spend real effort here.

4. CAUSA AND ITS ABSENCE. Civil systems differ on whether an act needs a cause/causa to be valid, and the proposal has a "telos" facet that looks superficially like cause but is not. Distinguish them rigorously. Does having a purpose facet create a hazard — i.e. will implementers conflate telos with causa and thereby draw wrong legal conclusions? Say what the spec must state to prevent that.
