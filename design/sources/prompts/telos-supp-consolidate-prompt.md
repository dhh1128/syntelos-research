Below are the outputs of several independent clustering passes. Each pass saw a different slice of
one corpus and no pass saw any other's output. Each grouped its slice by TELOS — the purpose an act
serves, the outcome that is its reason for existing.

You are given, separately, an EXISTING PARTITION of telos categories. It was derived from a
different corpus, which did not include the material these passes saw.

**Your task is not to re-derive a partition.** It is to answer one question: *what do these passes
contain that the existing partition cannot express?*

## How to work

Take each group from each pass in turn and decide which of three things it is:

1. **Covered** — an existing node's outward test is satisfied by the group's acts. Say which node.
   Different names for the same purpose are covered, not new; watch for the same name used for
   different things, and for different names used for the same thing. Both occur.
2. **Nearly covered** — the purpose plainly belongs with an existing node, but that node's outward
   test *as written* excludes the group's acts. This is a defect in the test, not a new root. Say
   which node, and what its test fails to reach.
3. **Not covered** — no existing node reaches it, and widening one to reach it would break that
   node's discriminating question against its neighbour.

Only category 3 produces a candidate new root, and the bar for it is high. Before proposing one,
try each existing node's test against the group honestly and say why it fails.

## The screens a candidate must survive

A candidate that fails any of these is not a root, however often it recurs.

- **Ultimate purpose.** A purpose that is always a means to some further end is not a root — strip
  it away and ask whether an end remains. "Represent a client" fails: the end is whatever is
  represented toward.
- **Not a standing relation.** Acting *on behalf of*, *in the capacity of*, *as agent for*
  describes who an actor is to someone else, not what they are trying to bring about. These belong
  on a different axis.
- **Not a mechanism.** "Read operations", "relocation", "record-keeping" name how something is
  done, not what for. A candidate that appears under many different purposes is a mechanism.
- **Not a subject area.** "Environmental acts", "financial acts" name what the object is, not what
  the act is for.
- **Externally checkable.** State the candidate's outward test as a yes/no question a stranger
  could decide from features observable **at the time the act is performed**. A test that requires
  reading anyone's mind is not a test.
- **No horizon claims.** The test may appeal to an outcome the act *determines* through mechanism.
  It may NOT appeal to an outcome that needs further acts, another party's uptake, or repetition
  before it obtains. Purposes like *persuade*, *build trust*, *motivate*, *reassure* all fail here:
  each names an effect in another party's head that the act does not determine. If your candidate's
  point is the reaction it produces in someone, it is a horizon and not a telos.

## Output

**A. Covered.** A compact table: input group name → existing node. One line each, no commentary
unless the mapping is surprising.

**B. Nearly covered — defects in existing tests.** Per item: the node, the acts its test fails to
reach, and the narrowest change to the test that would reach them without disturbing its
discriminating question. These are the most valuable findings in the exercise; do not skimp.

**C. Not covered — candidate roots.** Per candidate: a one-word lowercase name, a two-sentence
definition, its outward test in yes/no form, the discriminating question that separates it from its
nearest existing neighbour, roughly how many of the passes independently found it, and three
example act ids. State explicitly which existing tests you tried and why each failed.

**D. Rejected candidates.** Groups that looked like new roots and were screened out, with which
screen killed each. A candidate killed by the horizon screen is worth naming — that screen is the
one this exercise most often trips.

**E. What this corpus still cannot tell you.** Purposes a general taxonomy of acts would need that
no pass here exercised. Be concrete about what kind of corpus would exercise them.

Do not propose renaming any existing node — names are settled separately, and the labels below are
provisional handles for the semantics, not claims about the best word. Do not emit a per-act table.
Be dense; under 1,800 words.
