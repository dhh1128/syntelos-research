You are analyzing source material for a taxonomy design effort.

BACKGROUND. A standard called Syntelos defines a hierarchical taxonomy of digital INTERACTIONS classified by telos (purpose): eight roots — /relate, /share, /care, /serve, /align, /trade, /operate, /govern — with one level of children (e.g. /trade/lend, /govern/vote, /share/teach). Its declared scope is deliberately narrow: only interactions between two or more VOLITIONAL counterparties, each able to refuse, delay, or negotiate. Unilateral tool manipulation ("unlock the door", "write the file") is explicitly OUT of scope.

Syntelos is being considered for expansion so it can serve as a normative vocabulary for LEGAL ACTS — the acts a person, organization, or their delegate may perform with legal effect. Today, downstream systems use ad-hoc strings like "open-bank-account", "seat-the-board", "amend-operating-agreement", "declare-dividend".

THE MATERIAL. Appended are two things drawn from the Utah Code (the full statutory code of the U.S. state of Utah):
(A) a 1-in-14 systematic sample of the section headings ("catchlines") of every section of the code — 2,044 headings, each prefixed by its section number. A heading is the closest thing the statute has to a name for the act or subject the section governs. Headings use " -- " to join multiple subjects.
(B) a frequency table of the leading word of every heading segment across the FULL 28,606-heading population (not just the sample), so you can see the true distribution.

TASK. Answer with headers. Ground every claim in specific headings you quote with their section numbers.

1. WHAT KINDS OF THING DOES A STATUTE BOOK NAME? Classify the headings by what they are ABOUT. Statutes do not mostly name acts — they define terms, create bodies, impose duties, grant powers, prohibit conduct, set penalties, prescribe procedure, allocate revenue. Give a breakdown with rough percentages estimated from the sample plus the frequency table, and quote representative headings for each class. This is the most important section: it establishes how much of a legal code is actually act-shaped.

2. THE ACT CORPUS. From the act-shaped subset, extract a deduplicated list of 150-250 distinct LEGAL ACTS at consistent granularity (verb + object), e.g. "record a deed", "revoke a license", "serve process", "appoint a guardian". Group them into coherent families and name each family. Prefer acts a private party or their delegate could perform over acts only the state performs, but include both and mark which is which.

3. WHAT DIMENSIONS DISTINGUISH LEGAL ACTS? Derive, from the corpus, the axes along which legal acts actually differ. Test each of these candidates against the evidence and accept/reject/refine with quoted headings: (a) who may perform it — anyone, a licensed party, a fiduciary, only the state; (b) whether it changes a legal relation vs. operates within one; (c) Hohfeldian effect — creates/transfers/extinguishes a right, power, duty, or immunity; (d) formality — whether it requires writing, witnessing, notarization, filing, or registration to be effective; (e) whether it needs a counterparty's assent; (f) revocability; (g) whether it binds third parties. Propose axes the corpus reveals that are NOT in this list.

4. THE VOLITION TEST FAILS. Syntelos's in-scope test requires a counterparty who can refuse. Enumerate at least 15 acts from your corpus where this test gives the WRONG answer — acts that plainly need a normative name yet have no refusing counterparty (unilateral filings, self-executing elections, recordings, revocations, declarations, forbearances, omissions that are themselves legally operative). Quote the section headings. Then state, in one paragraph, what test SHOULD replace it if the taxonomy is to cover legal acts.

5. TELOS VS. EFFECT. Syntelos classifies by purpose. Legal acts are conventionally individuated by EFFECT and by INSTITUTIONAL FORM, not purpose — the same purpose ("protect my child") is served by a will, a trust, a guardianship petition, and an insurance policy, which are four different legal acts; and one legal form (a filing) serves dozens of purposes. Argue rigorously whether a purpose-rooted hierarchy can name legal acts at all, or whether purpose and legal-effect must be orthogonal facets. Take a definite position and give the strongest counterargument to it.

6. OMISSIONS AND DUTIES. Quantify how much of the code is about what someone MUST do or MUST NOT do rather than what they MAY do. Quote headings. State what this implies for a taxonomy whose current primitives are all permissions.

7. TRANSFERABILITY WARNING. Utah is one U.S. state. Say specifically which of your findings are artifacts of U.S. state law and would not survive translation to a civil-law jurisdiction or the EU, and which look jurisdiction-invariant.

RULES. Quote real headings with real section numbers; never invent one. If a claim is not supportable from the material, write "NOT IN SOURCE". Percentages must be labeled as estimates from the sample. Target 2000-3000 words; density over prose. Do not flatter the taxonomy — its scope rule is the thing under test.
