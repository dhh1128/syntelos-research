# Causal horizon, tree depth, and how deep a delegate may act on a person

Draft 2026-08-22, from Daniel's observation that several contested T1 boundaries are the same act
seen at different points on the proximate–ultimate continuum rather than different purposes.

## 1. The observation, tested

It holds for three of the nine contested concepts, and fails for the other six. The three it holds
for share a property.

**C3/C4 — knowing and teaching.** I explain a fact. Proximate: you know it. Ultimate: you are more
capable. One act, two horizons. `education`'s own discriminator gives it away — *"the end is a
changed person, not a produced fact"* — which describes a further point on the same causal chain,
not a different chain.

**C5/C6 — communicating and persuading.** I tell you something. Proximate: you received it.
Ultimate: your disposition changed. Persuasion is communication at a longer horizon. Same structure.

**C12/C13 — belonging and pairing.** Introduce two people (proximate) → they associate (medium) →
they marry (ultimate). `union`'s discriminator — *is a continuing bond the end?* — is a question
about how far down the chain the end sits.

**It does not hold for the rest.** C2 (accidental vs adversarial threat) differs in the *source of
harm*, and both terminate at the same point: harm did not occur. C8 (make vs maintain) differs in
*effect*, which is why it duplicates the effect facet. C10/C11 (test against a standard vs resolve a
contest) terminate differently rather than at different distances. C14 (ecosystems) is a subject
area. C15 (relocation) is an object class.

**What the three share:** every one of them is an act performed **on a person's mind or social
state**, where the act completes but its effect keeps unfolding in someone else. That is where the
continuum bites, and it is not an accident — for acts on inert things, the effect stops when the act
stops.

## 2. Why the derivation produced this, and what screen was missing

v1 already forbids it. §3.5: *make siblings out of categories that are roughly equivalent on the
proximate-ultimate causality continuum.* Knowing and teaching are not equivalent on it, so they
cannot be siblings.

The T2 screen I wrote after `representing` catches *"always a means to a further end."* It does not
catch *"the same act, named at a further horizon."* Those are different faults: `representing` was a
standing relation masquerading as a purpose; `education` is a real purpose located at the wrong
depth. **T2 needs a second screen** — for any two candidate roots, ask whether one is the downstream
effect of the other on the same causal chain. If so they are not siblings.

## 3. The resolution: horizon becomes depth, not sibling roots

Collapsing each pair to one root would throw away a real distinction. Keeping them as siblings
violates §3.5. The third option is the one v1's hierarchy was built for: **express the further
horizon as a child of the nearer one.**

    /inform                     tell someone something; success is receipt
    /inform/instruct            …so that they become capable; success is a changed competence
    /inform/instruct/form       …so that they become a certain kind of person

    /associate                  bring parties into contact
    /associate/match            …with a view to a continuing bond
    /associate/match/wed        …to the point of a formal, durable union

This uses hierarchy for exactly what v1 §3.2 claims it is good at — *confident generalisation*, where
a policy at `/x/y/*` covers `/x/y/z` unless overridden. And it inverts the default in the safe
direction: a grant of `/inform` does **not** carry `/inform/instruct`, because cascading runs from
general to specific and the specific here is the *deeper* horizon.

## 4. Why this matters more than taxonomy tidiness

Daniel's question: *may a delegate be told to instruct but not to persuade or to mould character?*

**Horizon is a stakes gradient.** The further down the causal chain a delegate is authorised to act
on a person, the more of that person's autonomy the delegate holds. Informing leaves the hearer's
judgment intact. Persuading engages it. Forming character replaces part of it. That is why the
distinction feels real despite being a continuum — it tracks exactly what `sda.md` §4 says gates
should track, and it is a *ladder*, not a partition.

So the answer is yes, and the ladder is the mechanism: a tutor holds `/inform/instruct`, a marketer
holds `/inform/persuade` under disclosure constraints, a guardian of a minor may hold
`/inform/instruct/form` where nobody else may, and a companion agent holding it would be a scandal.
These are grants at different depths of one branch, not different roots.

## 5. The enforcement problem, and where it is actually tractable

**The boundary cannot be checked at the utterance.** No inspection of a sentence distinguishes
informing from persuading; the same words do both depending on the hearer. And `sda.md` §1 already
concedes the general form of this: whether an act is *sincere* is not decidable from outside.

But it is checkable one level up. **What the delegate is optimising is inspectable, and it is the
real distinction.** An agent instructed to maximise engagement, conversion, or time-on-task *is*
persuading, structurally, whatever any individual utterance looks like. An agent optimising for
accurate transfer is informing. The objective is not a property of the transcript; it is a property
of the grant, and a grant is exactly the artifact this model can carry and a stranger can verify.

That reframes the enforcement question usefully:

- **Not** "did this message persuade?" — undecidable, and an invitation to censorship-shaped review.
- **But** "does this delegate hold an objective that only persuasion satisfies?" — answerable from
  the credential, before any message is sent.

This is the same move `sda.md` §4 makes for gates generally: do not try to check the heart, check the
category, and place the pause at the boundary. Here the boundary is the depth of the grant, and the
tell is the objective attached to it.

Two corollaries worth recording:

- **A duty can smuggle in depth a permission never granted.** The modality facet lets a principal
  impose a `must` with a goal and cadence. "Must improve completion rates" is a persuasion objective
  wearing an instruction permission. Depth of the *duty* must be checked against depth of the
  *permission*, or the ladder is defeated by the other axis.
- **Disclosure is the classic mitigation and it maps cleanly.** The relational statutes already do
  this — IMBRA requires a broker to disclose and to obtain consent on a *named* counterparty. That is
  a requisite (`noticed`, consent-gated) attached to a deep relational grant, which is precisely the
  shape this model predicts for a deep grant on a person.

## 6. What this changes downstream

- **T2** gains a second screen: same-chain-different-horizon, distinct from always-a-means.
- **T3** resolves C4, C6 and C13 by depth rather than by merge-or-split — which is a third answer the
  review document did not offer, and the review should be updated to offer it.
- **T6** must therefore write telos nodes with *depth*, not a flat root list. That is a change of
  shape: the other facets are flat, and telos is the one facet v1 always said was hierarchical.
- **The naming criteria gain a constraint**: a child's name must read as a narrowing of its parent,
  which is a set-level property like parallelism.
- **Open, and load-bearing:** does depth interact with the derived gate? If deeper grants on a person
  carry higher stakes, the gate function of `sda.md` §4 should read telos depth as an input, and at
  present it reads only effect, state-kind, and target. That is a real extension to the gate model,
  not a taxonomy detail.

## 7. The verification asymmetry — Daniel's objection, and it is fatal to §5 as written

§5 said the persuade/inform boundary is checkable in the grant rather than the transcript. That is
true and insufficient. A stranger reading the credential learns what was **authorised**. Open-loop
verification (`sda.md` §7) requires more than that: a stranger must be able to judge whether an act
falls inside the remit, from the credential and the act alone. For `/inform/instruct` versus
`/inform/instruct/form`, they cannot. Nothing in a tutoring session's outward behaviour distinguishes
instruction from character formation.

  [ F-8JQV ]  **Some branches of the telos tree are behaviourally unverifiable, and depth-on-persons
  is the worst case.** This is an asymmetry the tree does not currently acknowledge. Membership in
  `create`-a-`record` is checkable: the record exists. Membership in `/trade/swap` is checkable:
  value moved. Membership in `/inform/instruct/form` is not checkable at all, and — worse — the
  deeper the node, the less checkable it gets, so the tree's verifiability *decreases* exactly where
  its stakes *increase*.

That inverts the hierarchy's value proposition. v1 §3.2 sells hierarchy as confident generalisation
for policy, which presumes category membership is decidable. On this branch it is not.

### 7.1 What such a node actually is

If a stranger cannot check membership, the node is not an authorisation category. Continuing to
treat it as one is the error. It is a **disclosure-and-liability** category: it records what the
principal consented to, and it places liability if the delegate turns out to have gone deeper. Its
violation is discovered ex post — by the subject, by outcome, by complaint — not prevented ex ante by
a gate.

That is not a retreat; it is `sda.md` §1's own bound applied honestly. *Name the category, gate the
boundary, audit the remainder, and do not pretend to check the heart.* Character formation belongs
in the audited remainder. What was wrong was implying a gate could hold it.

### 7.2 Gate the affordances, not the influence

The compensating move is `sda.md` §7's containment-by-construction: do not check whether the
orchestrator is faithful, give it an empty act surface. Applied here — do not try to detect moulding,
**withhold the conditions that make moulding possible**, all of which are checkable:

- **persistence** — does the delegate retain memory of this subject across sessions? A one-off
  explanation cannot form character; a daily companion can.
- **curation** — does the delegate choose what the subject sees, or only answer what is asked?
- **exit** — can the subject leave, and is leaving costly?
- **metric-on-subject** — is the delegate optimising anything measured *on this person*? This is the
  sharpest one, and it is a property of the grant.
- **asymmetry** — is the subject a minor, a patient, a dependant, or otherwise unable to appraise
  the influence?

None of these *is* character formation. Together they are its enabling conditions, and each is
verifiable from the credential and the deployment rather than from the transcript. A tutor with no
persistent subject memory, no sequencing control, and no metric on the student cannot mould much,
whatever its intent — which is the point.

### 7.3 For acts on persons, the subject is the natural verifier

Where a stranger cannot judge, someone inside can. For acts *on a person*, that someone is the person
acted upon, and the model already has the machinery: proof requests, consent-gating, and the
`noticed` requisite.

The relational statutes independently arrived at exactly this. IMBRA gates a broker's acts on
disclosure to, and consent from, a **named** counterparty — not on any external assessment of the
broker's intentions. That is the shape this analysis predicts for a deep grant on a person, arrived
at by legislatures with the same problem and no taxonomy.

So the verification story for this branch is: **stranger checks the affordances; subject checks the
conduct; the record carries the liability.**

### 7.4 Consequence for the registry

  [ R-5MTN ]  **Telos nodes must carry a verifiability property**, and it must be honest.
  Something like `externally-checkable` / `subject-attestable` / `audit-only`, stated per node. An
  implementer needs to know which grants can be enforced at a gate and which can only be disclosed
  and audited, and today nothing in the model tells them apart. This is a new node field, and it
  probably generalises beyond telos — but telos is where it first becomes unavoidable.

### 7.5 The honest limit

None of this eliminates the problem. A delegate holding every legitimate affordance — persistent
memory, curation, a captive subject — can mould while sincerely reporting instruction, and no
verifier will catch it from outside. The claim is narrowing, not closure: make the deep act require
affordances that must be granted explicitly, make the subject a verifier where one exists, and let
the record place liability when it surfaces. A model that claimed more would be lying, which is the
standard `sda.md` §8 sets for itself.
